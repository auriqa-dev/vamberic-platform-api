# Shared CRM foundation — reviewed proposal

The subsequent [commercial foundation schema v3 proposal](commercial-foundation.md) separates generic capabilities, scoped instances, workspace packages and charges, adding eight collections and twenty-three indexes including Partner/membership/assignment relationships and workspace integrations without rewriting CRM schema v2 records.

See [application, actor and workspace authorization](application-authorization.md) for the enforced API boundary and future membership/service contracts.

Target: dev `vamberic_studio`, additive version 1 → 2. **Nothing has been applied.** This review supersedes the initial 18-collection / 72-index proposal. The final proposal has **19 domain collections and 61 application-declared indexes** (54 existing + 7 new). Counts exclude Mongo's automatic `_id` indexes and the `schema_versions` control collection.

## Architecture review — 2026-09-27

**Hive is the shared data platform. HVMapp is HVM's operational system. Vapp is portfolio/operator oversight and reporting.** Marketing agents belong operationally to HVM, not Vapp. Storing records together in Hive confers no operational or legal ownership rights.

Three concepts must remain distinct: Hive physically stores the data; an authorized operating application such as HVMapp may operate a workspace; the client may legally control its data. workspaceId identifies a neutral operational/client boundary. clientOrganisationId identifies the client context, not a statement of legal title or a data-access grant. References below to “ownership” mean operational record scope unless explicitly stated otherwise. Human `owner` is accountable CRM responsibility, not legal ownership.

### Workspace model: unchanged

The existing fields are sufficient for this tranche: `id`, `name`, `kind: portfolio | client`, `clientOrganisationId` (required for client, absent for portfolio), `archived`/`archivedAt`, `createdAt`/`updatedAt`, `createdBy`/`updatedBy`, `schemaVersion` and optional `source`. No parent workspace is allowed. Multiple workspaces may reference the same client organisation; no unique-client constraint exists.

`kind` already provides the current workspace-type distinction, so no duplicate workspaceType is added. Archive metadata supplies the current lifecycle; no speculative provisioning/status state machine is added. An operatingContext field is not required while workspace operations remain disabled: the future trusted authorization/application configuration must bind HVMapp/HVM actors to permitted workspaces. A mutable context label would not grant rights. Other operational contexts can be introduced deliberately when a consumer exists, without inventing enum values now.

Odyssiant Ltd can be a client organisation for an “Odyssiant HVM marketing account” workspace, with multiple separately registered Products/offers and campaigns. Built Matters can independently be a portfolio Product and the client context of a marketing workspace. HVM can have its own client workspace. These are examples only: no organisations, Products, offers or workspaces are seeded or inferred.

### Product and workspace scope matrix

All future client operations require an explicit workspaceId. Omission remains the legacy internal portfolio scope only; it is never an alternate way to access a client. Generic workspace writes remain blocked pending authorization.

| Collection            | Workspace context                                                                | Product context                                                                                                                        |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| crm_leads             | Required by schema.                                                              | Optional; qualification does not need a known Product.                                                                                 |
| product_relationships | Explicit for future client records; absent only for legacy internal data.        | Required because this specifically records a commercial relationship to a Product; do not create it just to hold an unclassified lead. |
| opportunities         | Required for client data; without workspace, legacy Product context is required. | Optional with workspace; may coexist. Organisation remains required.                                                                   |
| campaigns             | Required for client data; without workspace, legacy Product context is required. | Optional with workspace; may coexist.                                                                                                  |
| crm_pipelines         | Required for client data; without workspace, legacy Product context is required. | Optional with workspace; one workspace pipeline can support several Products.                                                          |
| crm_tasks             | Required for client work; legacy internal subjects remain compatible.            | Optional; person/organisation/opportunity can be the subject.                                                                          |

Workspace, Organisation, Product and Offer are distinct concepts. There is no Offer collection or offer implementation in this tranche. Lack of an offerId must remain unknown, not be filled with a workspace or fabricated Product. No one-to-one workspace/Product constraint exists; many campaigns, pipelines and optionally Product-linked records can share a workspace.

### Lead Gen is upstream of CRM

Future Lead Gen research may hold large populations of organisation/person/contact candidates, source evidence, signals, ICP fit and in-market intent. Candidate → researched → qualified → sales-ready is an upstream research progression, **not an automatic sequence of CRM inserts**. Raw crawls, unverified identities, discovery queues, complete research evidence stores and research scoring belong outside CRM in a later design. No research collections are created here.

Promotion occurs when an authorized operator or HVM workflow determines a prospect is commercially meaningful for a specific workspace and deliberately accepts it into CRM, or hands it to an external CRM instead. Promotion should resolve identity, scope and evidence; record the reason/source and an event; preserve unknown permission; and be idempotent. There is no automatic promotion/import workflow implemented now.

crm_leads already requires a canonical person/organisation subject and workspace, contains bounded qualification evidence and does not store arbitrary research candidates. No new promotion flags, mandatory invented scores, threshold or lifecycle enum is necessary. `leadStatus: researching` means follow-up investigation of an already accepted commercial lead, not storage of every discovered candidate; `new` means newly accepted into CRM. Qualification fields remain optional where facts are unknown. This is a documented ingestion boundary, not a claim that the schema can prove a lead's commercial merit. Future adapters must enforce promotion decisions. crm_leads changes only through the shared human-owner restriction below.

### Operational use and reporting

The future flow is **raw workspace data → authorized HVMapp/HVM-agent operations → authorized reporting projection/summary → Vapp oversight**. The schema does not require Vapp to query raw client contacts, research, campaign configuration, ICP definitions, CRM editing or agent controls. Current Vapp endpoints/projections explicitly exclude workspace records and stay unchanged in this review.

A future reporting projection may expose client/account status, active campaigns, leads generated, opportunities/pipeline value, advertising spend, agent/swarm activity, approvals, HVM revenue and account health. It needs defined metrics, attribution, access scope, freshness and suitable aggregation/redaction; it should not grant Vapp access to the underlying raw records. No reporting projection collection or UI is needed for schema correctness now, and none is added.

### Sharing and agents

Isolation is the default. Related-company status, shared Hive storage, equal domains or linked Products never authorize cross-workspace use. Future `workspace_data_grants` (a concept only, no collection) could specify source/destination workspaces, data/object scope, purpose, permitted applications/actions, approval, expiry and revocation. Odyssiant/HVM or Built Matters sharing requires such explicit permission; external clients receive no implicit exception. Existing product references and the internal client-registration reference are context links, not grants to traverse or operate another workspace.

Marketing agents perform actions for HVM. People and organisations have no agent-owner field. Lead, legacy product qualification and opportunity `owner` now accepts only `{type: human, id}` or omission; an agent/integration/system cannot be the CRM account owner. Workspace scope remains the operational data boundary independently of this human responsibility field. No leadGenAgentId/contentAgentId fields are added.

Existing actor metadata is sufficient: a human, including a person operating as a Swarm Queen, uses `{type: human, id}`; a registered agent uses `{type: agent, id}`; and automation uses `{type: system, id}`. Swarm Queen is the external Partner business role, not an agent category. The Partner business ID is never substituted for the actual human actor. Future trusted membership and assignment resolution determines permissions. Tasks can be assigned to those actors; events/createdBy/updatedBy record their actions. Self-reported actor metadata does not authorize access.

Only the proposed Zod owner validation changed in this review. No workspace fields, research/reporting/sharing collections, HTTP contracts, Mongo validators or indexes were added or changed. The final collection and index inventory remains 19 / 61.

## Events, interactions and work

The existing `events` model already has an extensible eventType, occurredAt, product/person/organisation/campaign links, source/audit metadata and payload. It can represent email, call, meeting, note, LinkedIn/SMS/WhatsApp communications, form submissions, campaign touches, qualification/lifecycle/opportunity changes and agent actions. A second historical interaction collection would duplicate its responsibility without a synchronization rule.

**`crm_activities` has been removed from the unapplied proposal**, including its ID prefix, collection, validator, indexes and write path. `events` is the single canonical historical stream. Optional `interaction` supplies bounded subject/body/summary, actor, typed call/email/meeting/note/communication details and attachment references. Optional personIds, opportunityId and taskId provide structured subjects. Legacy eventType/payload records, including public enquiry events, remain valid and are not copied or backfilled. Communication channel identifies LinkedIn, SMS, WhatsApp or other channels without creating another collection.

Events are append-only through the foundation service: updates are rejected. Existing narrow hard deletion of disposable public-enquiry test graphs remains an explicit exception; enriched interaction history blocks that deletion. Internal reference-conflict counters are concurrency metadata, not edits to historical event content. Correction and subsequent facts should be separate events, not rewrites.

`crm_tasks` holds current actionable work: assignment, due date, priority and pending/in_progress/completed/cancelled state. Overdue is derived. A planned call is a task; the call that actually happened is an event and may link taskId. Completing a task does not fabricate evidence that an interaction occurred. No automatic task-to-interaction mirror or duplicate activity stream exists. Scheduling, outreach delivery, task-transition event automation and provider ingestion/idempotency remain future consumers, not features in this tranche. Importers must resolve external mappings before appending provider events; this proposal does not claim retry-safe ingestion already exists.

## Ownership and application boundaries

The previous implementation had **no workspace/client ownership model** and was product-bound. It was insufficient for external HVM clients.

The revised model adds `crm_workspaces` with platform workspace ID, name, kind (portfolio/client), optional clientOrganisationId and archive/source/audit metadata. A client workspace requires a client organisation reference; a portfolio workspace does not. This is client registration, not a grant to read that organisation's other records. Workspace roots cannot nest or have their kind/client ownership reassigned through the service. The client registry organisation is internal portfolio data; operational copies of organisations/people in a client workspace have their own IDs.

Every persistence record can carry workspaceId. **Absent workspaceId has exactly one compatibility meaning: existing internal Vamberic portfolio data. It never means public, shared, all clients or an inferred HVM client.** Null/invalid workspace IDs fail Zod validation. No implicit backfill or client assignment is performed. For explicitly owned records, workspaceId resolves to one workspace; organisation, person, contact, employment, lead, campaign, pipeline, opportunity, task, event, import, permission and external-mapping links must stay in that workspace. The new foundation reference checker rejects cross-workspace links for its supported CRM writes; future import/billing/permission adapters must uphold the same ownership invariant before any client-scoped operations are enabled. Product references are commercial context, not ownership or access grants.

Current Vapp CRM lists/details and related projections exclude all workspace-owned records. Public enquiries only look up internal records and cannot reuse/modify client identities. Vapp hard-delete refuses workspace-owned targets/dependencies. Generic foundation writes reject workspace operations, including attempted reassignment of legacy records, until a trusted application authorization boundary exists. Exact-scope permission resolution now includes workspaceId; a client's consent cannot become another client's or portfolio permission. Public forms cannot mass-assign scope.

This is ownership preparation and a fail-closed application boundary, **not a complete authorization system**. Direct Mongo access is privileged and is not tenant-isolated by these schemas. HVMapp must later receive explicitly authorized application/actor/workspace access; Vapp reporting requires a separately authorized aggregate projection. Neither is enabled here. Before accepting external client data, implement authorization across every read/write/import path, workspace-scoped ingestion/dedupe indexes and permission precedence. Existing public-enquiry unique indexes are unchanged and that ingestion source remains internal-only.

Odyssiant → HVM-own-marketing or Built Matters related-company sharing will require an explicit, auditable grant describing source/destination workspaces, application, purpose, allowed objects/actions and revocation. No sharing flags or grants are invented now; external clients remain isolated by default. Equal domains, email addresses, Product IDs or providerAccountIds never imply sharing.

## Product versus client context

A Product is an offering in the Vamberic portfolio, not an HVM client. A workspace owns operational CRM data; its client organisation can represent Built Matters, Odyssiant, HVM itself or an external client. Examples are conceptual, not seeded records. One client can have multiple offerings. Optional productId links an actual registered portfolio Product when appropriate; do not manufacture Products for client ownership or unregistered offers.

`crm_leads` holds workspace-specific qualification and sales lifecycle, with required workspaceId, person and/or organisation, optional product/campaign, owner, attribution, next action and evidence. It is deliberately separate from `product_relationships`, which retains commercial relationship status and its required Product. Existing optional product qualification fields remain compatible for internal legacy use; new client qualification belongs in crm_leads and is not dual-written. Qualification and commercial customer state answer different questions.

Campaigns, opportunities and pipelines accept workspace context without a Product. Internal legacy campaigns/opportunities/pipelines still require productId. Opportunities continue to require an organisation: consumer opportunities remain a separate model decision. Existing HTTP contracts are unchanged; current Vapp projections remain internal-only.

`crm_pipelines` is configurable process metadata: stable stage IDs, labels, ordering, mapped opportunity status and optional probability. It is owned by a workspace, optionally narrowed to a Product; legacy internal pipelines are Product-specific. A workspace-wide pipeline can serve opportunities for multiple optional Products in that same workspace. Stage IDs/orders are unique, probabilities are 0..1, selected stages/status must match, and edits cannot invalidate existing opportunities. No pipeline or HubSpot stage has been seeded.

## External references

`external_references` maps provider + providerAccountId + objectType + externalId to a typed platform entity. Provider is a bounded string, supporting HubSpot, Lusha, Paddle, LinkedIn/ad platforms, marketplaces and other systems without canonical provider-specific ID additions. Existing legacy provider fields remain compatible; future adapters use mappings rather than adding more such fields. Targets include CRM objects, workspaces, events, imports and billing records.

Uniqueness is **(workspaceId, provider, providerAccountId, objectType, externalId)**. All external identity components are required strings; workspaceId is absent only for legacy internal scope and Mongo indexes it as the missing/null key component. Application validation rejects explicit null. Different workspaces/accounts can hold separately authorized mappings, but that fact does not authorize sharing. One external identity has one target within its workspace/account/object scope. The nonunique (entityType, entityId) reverse index supports mapping lookup and conservative deletion checks. Platform IDs remain globally unique. Source creation/modification times are distinct from local audit times.

## Profile fields, evidence and writes

People add preferredLanguage, timezone and validated social profiles. Contact points add label, source verification time/reason and an optional employment link. Organisations add description, website, normalizedDomain, address, industryGroup, foundedYear, LinkedIn URL, parent, revenue band, annualRevenue (minor units + explicit ISO currency + observedAt) and labelled telephone; size fields are reused. Employment adds employmentRole. Qualification fields include lifecycle/lead status, owner/assignment time, targetAccount, icpTier, persona, reasons, next action and first/latest attribution. Opportunities add description, pipeline, owner, priority, deal type, won reason and bounded buying roles validated against linked people.

Bounded field evidence retains allowlisted path, typed value, provider/source reference, observedAt, verification and confidence. Observed/rejected candidates never replace facts; verified candidates only fill absent fields. Existing values require explicit human edits. Human verification requires a human actor; original source and evidence history cannot be rewritten. Unknown legacy fields and nested siblings survive targeted updates. Writes validate merged Zod records and references inside transactions, use updatedAt compare-and-swap and reference-conflict counters. This internal service is not a public mass-assignment API.

Permissions remain in marketing_permissions. Unknown permission stays unknown; enrichment/discovery/contact availability never creates consent. Exact-scope resolution is not a complete outreach eligibility policy. No scheduler, outreach execution, guessed lifecycle, currency, owner, fit score or portfolio-wide permission is introduced.
